1. Infancy & Childhood

Focus on psychosocial development, attachment and how children use relationships to explore their environment.

重點是兒童如何透過照顧關係建立安全感、自主感及能力感。

Erikson: psychosocial development

A psychosocial crisis is a developmental tension involving the person and social expectations. It does not necessarily mean a psychiatric emergency.

Crisis 在此可指正常發展中的張力，不一定是嚴重創傷或精神科危機。 處理階段任務可促進一種基本能力，稱為 virtue。

Period Central tension Helpful idea
Infancy Trust vs mistrust Can caregivers be relied on? Virtue: hope.
Toddlerhood Autonomy vs shame and doubt Can I try to do things myself? Virtue: will.
Early childhood Initiative vs guilt Can I initiate activities and plans? Virtue: purpose.
School age Industry vs inferiority Can I learn and feel capable? Virtue: competence.
Example: A toddler tries to dress independently. A caregiver gives time and appropriate help. This supports autonomy.

幼兒嘗試自己穿衣，照顧者給予時間和適當幫助，有助建立自主感。

Exam tip: Identify the central concern in the case. Age is useful, but behaviour and context provide important evidence too.

Attachment theory

Attachment concerns a child's relationship with a caregiver, particularly how the child seeks safety and comfort when distressed. A secure base allows the child to explore and return for support when needed.

依附關注孩子在需要保護或安慰時如何與照顧者互動。 安全基地讓孩子有信心探索，也能在不安時回來尋求支持。

Four commonly described patterns
  • Secure: may be upset by separation; seeks comfort on reunion and can return to exploration.
  • Avoidant: tends to avoid or minimise contact on reunion. Little outward distress does not prove no distress.
  • Resistant / ambivalent: seeks proximity but may resist comfort and remain distressed.
  • Disorganized: behaviour may appear contradictory, frozen, dazed or disoriented.
Do not over-infer: One behaviour or one brief observation is not enough to diagnose an attachment pattern or determine a caregiver's history.

單一行為不足以診斷依附類型，也不能直接推斷照顧者一定曾虐待或忽視。

Social referencing & development

Social referencing: a child looks to a trusted person's emotional reaction when deciding how to respond to an unfamiliar situation.

Example: A child sees a new toy, looks at a caregiver's face, and then approaches after seeing an encouraging expression.

孩子參考照顧者的情緒反應，協助理解陌生情境。

Brain development and social referencing are useful extensions from the childhood material; confirm their detailed exam scope with the course materials.

2. Adolescence

Focus on identity development, the difference between exploration and commitment, and the two dimensions of parenting.

重點是青少年如何探索「我是誰」，以及如何區分不同身分認同狀態。

Erikson: identity vs role confusion

Adolescents work to develop a coherent sense of self while negotiating belonging, values, relationships and future roles.

青少年要整合個人價值、群體歸屬和未來方向，逐步形成自我認同。

Marcia: identity formation

Ask two separate questions:

  1. Exploration: Is the person actively considering meaningful alternatives?
  2. Commitment: Has the person made a personally invested choice?
Status Exploration Commitment
Diffusion Low / absent Low / absent
Foreclosure Little or none Present
Moratorium Active Absent or not yet firm
Achievement Exploration has occurred Present
Foreclosure vs moratorium: foreclosure = a commitment without exploration; moratorium = active exploration without a firm commitment.

早閉型：已決定但未探索。未定型：正在探索但未確定。

Example: “My family chose accounting for me; I did not consider other careers” points towards foreclosure. “I am comparing accounting and nursing” points towards moratorium if no firm decision has been made.

A person's status may differ across domains, such as occupation and beliefs. A decision alone does not show whether exploration occurred.

Baumrind: parenting styles

Compare demandingness—rules and expectations—and responsiveness—warmth and attention to the child's views.

Style Demandingness Responsiveness
Authoritative High High
Authoritarian High Low
Permissive Low High
Neglectful Low Low
Authoritative vs authoritarian: both can involve rules. The difference is the degree of warmth, explanation and responsiveness.

兩者都可能有規則；威信型會解釋並聆聽，專制型則較強調服從。

Self-concept & self-esteem

Self-concept refers to how a person describes or understands themselves. Self-esteem concerns evaluation of one's own worth.

Self-concept 是「我如何描述自己」；self-esteem 是「我如何評價自己的價值」。 這是青春期講義的延伸內容，請按課程範圍複習。

3. Adulthood

Focus on intimacy, generativity, love and moral reasoning.

重點是成人期關係、對下一代的關懷，以及道德判斷背後的理由。

Erikson: adult tasks

  • Intimacy vs isolation: developing close relationships while maintaining a sense of self.
  • Generativity vs stagnation: caring for, guiding or contributing to others and future generations.

Generativity 不只限於生育；指導年輕人、照顧他人或貢獻社區也可能表現這個概念。

Sternberg: triangular theory of love

  • Intimacy: closeness, warmth, connection and trust.
  • Passion: romantic or physical attraction and desire.
  • Commitment: a decision to love and maintain the relationship.
Type Components Quick reminder
Liking Intimacy Close, without passion or commitment.
Infatuation Passion Strong attraction alone.
Empty love Commitment Decision to remain, without closeness or passion.
Romantic love Intimacy + passion Close and attracted; commitment not established.
Companionate love Intimacy + commitment Close and committed; passion is limited.
Fatuous love Passion + commitment Attraction and commitment, little intimacy.
Consummate love All three Intimacy, passion and commitment.
Exam strategy: Identify which components are present before naming the type. Do not infer a component that the case does not describe.

先找出親密、激情、承諾是否出現，再選類型；不要自行補上題目沒有的資訊。

Kohlberg: moral reasoning

The key question is why a person makes a judgement, not simply whether the answer is “yes” or “no.”

Level Stage Reasoning focus
Pre-conventional 1. Punishment Avoid punishment or obey authority.
Pre-conventional 2. Exchange Personal interests and reciprocal benefit.
Conventional 3. Relationships Be a good person; meet others' expectations.
Conventional 4. Social order Follow rules and duties to maintain order.
Post-conventional 5. Social contract Protect rights; laws can be revised through agreement.
Post-conventional 6. Universal principles Follow internalised ethical principles, even against rules.
Stage 5 vs stage 6: Stage 5 emphasises rights, social agreement and revisable laws. Stage 6 emphasises an internalised universal ethical principle.

第 5 階段重社會契約與權利；第 6 階段重個人內化、適用於所有人的倫理原則。

4. Late Adulthood

Focus on life review, adaptation, functioning and the distinction between SOC strategies.

重點是晚年如何看待人生、適應改變及運用有限資源。

Erikson: ego integrity vs despair

  • Ego integrity: finding coherence and acceptance in one's life, including imperfect experiences.
  • Despair: experiencing profound regret or a sense that life cannot be made meaningful.
  • Virtue: wisdom.

自我圓滿不代表一生沒有錯誤，而是能承認遺憾，仍找到整體意義。

Life review: A worker invites an older client to reflect on meaningful events and unresolved concerns, without forcing a positive interpretation.

SOC: selective optimization with compensation

SOC describes strategies for pursuing meaningful goals when resources are limited or changing. It is not restricted to older adulthood.

Strategy Main question Example
Elective selection Which goal matters most by preference? Choose music as a priority without a recent loss.
Loss-based selection Which goal should change after resource loss? Reduce activities after stamina declines.
Optimization How can skills or resources improve goal pursuit? Practise regularly to improve performance.
Compensation What alternative means can maintain the goal? Use a magnifier after vision loss to keep reading.
Selection vs compensation: changing or prioritising goals after loss suggests loss-based selection; keeping a goal but using an alternative means suggests compensation.

改變目標或優先次序：loss-based selection； 維持目標但改用方法：compensation。

Peck: additional late-adulthood concepts

These concepts are developed in Lecture 9 and are supplementary to the clearly visible Lecture 10 overview.

  • Ego differentiation: find self-worth beyond a former work role.
  • Body transcendence: find enjoyment and meaning beyond physical limitations, while still attending to health needs.
  • Ego transcendence: face mortality constructively and find meaning beyond one's individual lifespan.

分別是：超越工作角色、超越身體限制，以及以建設性方式面對生命有限。

Age & everyday functioning

  • Chronological age: age in years.
  • Biological age: condition of body systems.
  • Psychological age: how a person acts and feels.
  • Functional age: how a person performs daily roles.

ADLs: basic personal care, such as eating, dressing, bathing and toileting.

IADLs: activities supporting independent living, such as shopping, managing money and managing medication.

實際能力不能只靠出生年份推斷。ADL 是基本個人照顧； IADL 是支持獨立生活的工具性活動。

5. Social Identity, Power & Privilege

Focus on how social identities relate to power, access, advantage and disadvantage.

重點不只是列出身分，而是理解身分如何與社會權力和資源分配相連。

Key distinctions

  • Social identity: a social position or group membership, such as gender, ethnicity, age or disability status.
  • Stereotype: a generalised belief about a group.
  • Prejudice: an attitude or judgement towards people based on group membership.
  • Discrimination: unfair treatment related to group membership or social identity.
  • Oppression: a broader pattern of disadvantage maintained through power, institutions and social practices.
Example: Assuming all older applicants cannot learn software is a stereotype. Refusing a qualified applicant an interview because of age is an example of discrimination.

想法與行動要分清：群體概括是刻板印象；具體不公平待遇是歧視。

Privilege & the coin model

Privilege refers to advantages associated with a social position in a particular context. A person may have advantages in one area while facing barriers in another.

The coin model encourages us to examine how advantage and oppression are connected within systems, rather than treating inequality only as individual intentions.

Coin model 把 privilege 和 oppression 放在同一社會關係中理解。 有優勢不代表該人一定有歧視意圖。

Example: A high-income wheelchair user may afford private transport but still face inaccessible public buildings. Economic advantage and disability-related barriers can coexist.

Power & professional reflexivity

Social workers may control information, assessments or access to services. Reflexivity means considering how one's role and assumptions affect the professional relationship.

社工應反思自己的專業權力，並留意服務使用者是否有空間表達不同意見。

Evidence matters: An unsuccessful application alone does not prove or rule out discrimination. Explore the criteria, process and the person's account.

6. Ecological Theory & Intersectionality

Focus on the interaction between people and environments, and on how multiple social positions connect with structural conditions.

先確認「誰是分析對象」，再判斷環境是直接、間接，還是不同環境之間的連結。

Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems

Level Key idea Example for a child
Person The active, developing individual. The child's health, preferences and experiences.
Microsystem A direct setting and interactions within it. The child's classroom or family interaction.
Mesosystem Links between direct settings. Parent and teacher coordinate support.
Exosystem An indirect setting affecting the person. A parent's workplace changes shifts.
Macrosystem Broad cultural and institutional patterns. Social beliefs and wider policy context.
Chronosystem Time, transitions and historical change. Migration and school changes over several years.
Focal person matters: A workplace is a microsystem for a parent who works there, but may be an exosystem for the child who never attends yet is affected by the parent's working hours.
Micro vs meso vs exo: direct setting → microsystem; link between direct settings → mesosystem; indirect setting → exosystem.

Lecture 10: micro–mezzo–macro

  • Micro: individual biological, psychological, social and cognitive characteristics.
  • Mezzo: interpersonal environment, such as family, friends, school and workplace relationships.
  • Macro: structural environment, such as policy, regulation and law.
Important: These labels are not identical to Bronfenbrenner's labels. A child's direct interaction with a teacher is a microsystem interaction in Bronfenbrenner's model, but the interpersonal environment is mezzo in Lecture 10's three-level approach.

這兩套框架的用字不同，不要把 mezzo 和 mesosystem 當作同義詞。

Intersectionality

Multiple social identities and positions interact with interpersonal and structural systems to shape experiences. Intersectionality is more than simply adding up identity labels.

Example: A worker explores how gender, disability and income interact with hiring practices and transport access.

交織性不只問「當事人有多少種身分」，還要問這些身分如何與制度及權力互動。

A single-axis analysis may miss people whose experiences occur at the intersection of more than one social position.

Iceberg thinking

A visible behaviour or presenting problem is a starting point. Explore what is happening now, what has happened over time, and possible individual, relational and structural influences.

不要只看「冰山一角」；但也不能未經評估就假設隱藏原因。

7. Stress Response

Focus on stressor vs stress, the three response categories, bodily adaptation and ACE scoring.

重點是區分外在壓力源、個人壓力經驗、身體反應及童年逆境。

Stressor, stress & stress response

  • Stressor: an external event or circumstance that challenges adaptation.
  • Stress: a subjective state associated with appraising demands as exceeding available resources.
  • Stress response: physiological and behavioural responses that help the body deal with a challenge.
Example: Job loss is a stressor. Worry that savings will not cover rent is part of the person's stress experience. Increased heart rate can be part of a stress response.

Three response categories in the lecture

Category Key idea Support / context
Positive stress A challenge is managed and can build mastery. Usually manageable with available resources.
Tolerable stress A serious negative event is buffered. Adequate personal or social support is available.
Toxic stress Prolonged or severe adversity may produce excessive activation. Especially concerning when adequate support is absent.
Do not classify from the event name alone. Consider severity, duration, frequency, controllability and support. One ACE does not automatically prove toxic stress.

不能只憑事件名稱判斷；要看持續時間、程度和支持。 一個 ACE 不自動等於 toxic stress。

Homeostasis, allostasis & allostatic load

  • Homeostasis: maintaining physiological balance within a workable range.
  • Allostasis: adaptive changes that help the body meet demands and manage threats.
  • Allostatic load: cumulative physiological burden associated with repeated or prolonged adaptation.
Example: A temporary rise in heart rate during danger is an adaptive response. Repeated or prolonged activation may contribute to cumulative physiological burden.

短期調節不等於損害；長期反覆調節才可能增加累積負荷。

SNS, HPA & hormones

  • SNS: the sympathetic nervous system contributes to a rapid response, including increased heart rate and breathing.
  • Adrenaline: associated with rapid mobilisation during the response.
  • HPA axis: hypothalamus → pituitary → adrenal glands.
  • Cortisol: participates in stress adaptation and helps increase glucose availability.

SNS 與快速反應及腎上腺素有關；HPA 軸涉及下丘腦、腦下垂體、 腎上腺及 cortisol。兩套系統會互相影響。

Brain reminders: the amygdala is involved in threat-related emotion; the hippocampus is important for memory. These structures work within wider networks.

ACEs: conventional ten-category framework

ACEs are a subset of childhood adversity. In this conventional home-based framework, score each supported category once.

Abuse and neglect: five categories
  1. Physical abuse
  2. Emotional abuse
  3. Sexual abuse
  4. Physical neglect
  5. Emotional neglect
Household challenges: five categories
  1. Parental separation or divorce
  2. Household substance misuse
  3. Household mental illness
  4. Violence against the mother
  5. Incarcerated household member
Scoring rule: Count categories, not the number of incidents. Count only what the case supports. If other categories have not been assessed, the complete score may be unknown.

同一類發生多次仍計一分；不同類別各計一分。 題目沒有提供的經歷不可自行推定。

Example: Emotional abuse + physical neglect + household substance misuse = 3 categories.

Broader adversity: bullying, community violence and discrimination can matter even when they are not included in the conventional ten-category score.

ACE 分數不是診斷，也不能決定一個人的未來。支持、時間、 事件的嚴重程度及其他經驗都重要。
